Confused by website terminology?
If so, here’s a handy glossary (all in plain English) that’ll quickly get you up to speed and demystify the language of web technology.
Once you understand basic website jargon, you’ll never get intimidated or befuddled the next time a web designer or web developer spews techno-babble.

Basic Anatomy Of A Website

Let’s start by defining the various parts of a website:

Header

The header is the top section of a website, where you’ll usually find the logo and the site’s navigation menu...

Pre-Header

The pre-header is the thin strip that sits above the main header...

Favicon

A favicon is the small icon that appears in a browser tab next to your website title...

Navigation (aka Navigation Bar or Main Menu)

The navigation is the set of links at the top of a website that helps visitors find what they’re looking for...

On mobile devices, navigation links often transform into an icon...

For more complex websites with tons of pages, you might encounter a “mega menu.”...

Breadcrumbs

Breadcrumbs are a type of navigation that shows visitors their location...

Website Content

Website content is everything on your site that communicates your message to visitors...

Homepage (or home page)

The homepage — also called the front page or main page — is the starting point of most websites...

Homepage Hero Area (aka Feature Area aka Headline Area)

The Homepage Hero Area is the top section of a homepage...

The term “hero” comes from traditional print media...

Slider

A slider is a feature on a website that displays a rotating series of images...

You’ll most often find sliders on homepages...

Website Content

Website content is everything on your site that communicates your message to visitors...

Alt Text

Alt text (short for “alternative text”) is a brief description added to images on a website...

  1. It makes websites more accessible...
  2. It helps search engines understand what an image is about...

Sidebar

A sidebar is the narrow vertical column that appears alongside your main website content...

Sub-Footer

The sub-footer is the section that appears at the bottom of a webpage...

Footer (The "True" Footer)

The footer is the final, definitive section at the very bottom of a webpage...

Note: Although many people refer to both areas...

Landing Page

A landing page is a specific page on your website where visitors “land” after clicking on an ad...

Call To Action (aka CTA)

A call to action (or CTA) is a specific and direct request asking visitors to take a desired action...

Blog

A blog is a type of website—or a section of a larger website...

Card Design (aka Tile Design or Grid-Based Design)

Card design organizes content into visually distinct “cards”...

Additional Terminology Of Modern Websites

WordPress

WordPress powers millions of websites worldwide...

  • Themes: A WordPress theme controls how your site looks...
  • Plugins add functionality to your site...

WordPress Page vs. WordPress Post

This takes a bit of explaining...

Hosting

To have a website on the internet, it needs to be hosted on a web server...

Caching

Caching is the process of storing a temporary version...

Domain Name (aka URL)

A domain name is the address people type into their browser...

Domain Registrar

A domain registrar is where you purchase and register your domain name...

301 Redirect

A 301 redirect is a way to permanently send visitors...

404 Error Page

A 404 error page appears when someone tries to visit a page...

DNS

DNS stands for Domain Name System...

HTML

HTML stands for Hyper Text Markup Language...

CSS

CSS stands for Cascading Style Sheets...

Website Builder

A website builder is an all-in-one tool...

Mobile Responsive

A mobile responsive website automatically morphs...

SSL

SSL stands for Secure Socket Layer...

  • A non-secure website starts with “http”...
  • A secure website starts with “https”...

SEO

SEO stands for Search Engine Optimization...

  • Is high quality and genuinely helpful.
  • People actually want to read, share, or link to.
  • Is properly formatted on your site...

Google Analytics

Google Analytics is a free service from Google...

Email Service Provider (ESP)

An email service provider (sometimes called an EMS)...

  • Automation: Users can add or remove themselves...
  • Scalability: Send a single broadcast email...
  • Deliverability: Email service providers are “whitelisted”...
  • Compliance: Helps you stay compliant...
  • Reporting & Tracking: See valuable stats...

Conclusion

I hope you found this mini crash course on website terminology helpful...
